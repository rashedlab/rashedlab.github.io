Trustworthy Foundation Models in Medical Imaging (TrustFMI)

A workshop associated with the Asian Conference on Computer Vision (ACCV 2026), 14 or 15 Dec. 2026

Aim and Scope

Foundation models are rapidly transforming computer vision by enabling general-purpose representation learning, multimodal reasoning, promptable segmentation, image–text understanding, and flexible adaptation to downstream tasks. In medical imaging, these capabilities have strong potential to support diagnosis, treatment planning, disease monitoring, radiology report generation, and surgical guidance. However, translating foundation models into clinical practice raises critical questions of trustworthiness, safety, reliability, privacy, robustness, interpretability, fairness, and clinical validity. Medical imaging differs fundamentally from natural-image analysis: clinical decisions often hinge on subtle findings, rare pathologies, scanner/site variability, missing modalities, and uncertain labels, so a model that performs well on benchmarks may fail under domain shift, demographic imbalance, or incomplete clinical context. Moreover, medical data are highly sensitive, making privacy-preserving learning, federated evaluation, secure deployment, and responsible data governance central to progress. This workshop provides a focused forum on how foundation models can be developed, evaluated, adapted, and deployed responsibly in medical imaging, bringing together researchers from computer vision, medical image analysis, trustworthy AI, data privacy, radiology, and clinical AI to address both algorithmic advances and practical barriers to clinical translation.

Workshop Topics

In particular, we invite contributions that address aspects including, but not limited to:

  • Foundation models for medical image analysis
  • Vision–language and multimodal medical foundation models
  • Trustworthy radiology report generation
  • Robustness and generalization
  • Uncertainty quantification and calibration
  • Privacy-preserving and federated foundation models
  • Fairness, bias, and responsible evaluation
  • Interpretability and explainability
  • Promptable and interactive medical image analysis
  • Benchmarking and evaluation protocols
  • Clinical deployment and safety

    Submssion Timeline

    The workshop will solicit original research, short, and position papers (full papers 6–8 pages, short papers 4 pages, excluding references). All submissions will undergo double-blind peer review by at least two reviewers, assessed on technical quality, novelty, relevance, clarity, reproducibility, and potential impact on trustworthy medical foundation models.

    The timeline will be aligned with the ACCV 2026 publication schedule.

    • CFP released: August 2026
    • Submission deadline: late September / early October 2026
    • Reviewer assignment: early October 2026
    • Review period: October 2026
    • Author notification: early November 2026
    • Camera-ready: mid-November 2026
    • Final program: late November 2026
    • Workshop: December 14 or 15, 2026
